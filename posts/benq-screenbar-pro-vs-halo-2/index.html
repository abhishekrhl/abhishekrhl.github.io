BenQ ScreenBar Pro vs ScreenBar Halo 2: which light bar?

Affiliate disclosure: Some links are affiliate links. As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases. See Disclosure.

Both of these clip on top of your monitor and light the desk without shining into your eyes. They share more than you’d guess: the same 500 lux figure, the same motion sensor range, the same curved-monitor support. The real split is the backlight and the controller. This comparison uses BenQ’s UK product pages, checked on 12 November 2026. We haven’t tested either bar ourselves.

The short answer

Get the ScreenBar Halo 2 if you often work in a dim or dark room. It adds a backlight behind the monitor to soften the contrast between a bright screen and a dark wall, and it comes with a wireless desk controller.

Get the ScreenBar Pro if your room is already reasonably lit and you just want good desk light that switches itself on and off. It’s the simpler, front-light-only option and runs on less power.

Where they’re the same

A few things often get described as differences when they aren’t.

Both bars use an ultrasonic sensor about 50 cm above the desk that watches a 60 ± 10 cm zone in front of the screen. Both can turn off when you walk away. BenQ is honest about the catch on both pages: if you sit very still, say watching a video, the light may go off after five minutes.

Both fit curved monitors from 1000R to 1800R. The Pro takes slightly thicker monitor tops (up to 6.5 cm against 6 cm).

Both were designed with the Chicago studio MINIMAL, and both use full-spectrum LEDs that BenQ rates at Rf above 96 for colour accuracy.

What the Halo 2 adds

The backlight. This is the main reason to pay more. BenQ says the Halo 2’s tri-zone backlight covers 423% more area than the first ScreenBar Halo. The aim is to stop the wall behind your monitor being a black hole while you stare at a bright screen. If you work late with the room lights off, this is the feature you’d notice.

A lower minimum. BenQ says the Halo 2 goes 64% dimmer at its lowest setting than the original Halo. That helps when even a dimmed light feels like too much at night.

The controller. A puck-style wireless controller sits on the desk with a numeric touch panel. One controller can run two Halo 2 bars, which suits a two-monitor setup. BenQ quotes up to three months between charges.

It also auto-adjusts brightness to the room, not just on and off.

What the Pro does well

It’s simpler. There’s no controller to charge, and the bar needs less power (BenQ lists 5V at up to 1.7A, against up to 3A for the Halo 2). It can run from a monitor’s USB-C port or from the adapter in the box. BenQ warns that if the monitor port can’t supply enough power, the light may dim, flicker or not work properly, so check your monitor’s USB-C output first. BenQ also says not to use USB-C to USB-A adapters with it.

BenQ also quotes a centre brightness above 1,000 lux and says the Pro is 34% brighter at maximum than the original ScreenBar (measured at a 45 cm monitor height). The lamp head tilts up to 24 degrees. And the cable is fixed to the bar on purpose: BenQ says that avoids wear from repeated plugging, and the cable passed a 1,200-swing test with a 0.3 kg weight.

Who should pick which

  • Halo 2 if you work after dark with the room lights low, or run two monitors and want one controller for both.
  • Pro if your room is lit, you want the light to look after itself, and you’d rather not have another gadget to charge.
