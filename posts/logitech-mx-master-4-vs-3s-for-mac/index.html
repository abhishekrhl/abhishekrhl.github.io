Logitech MX Master 4 vs MX Master 3S for Mac

We haven’t had either mouse on our desk for a long stretch, so this isn’t a hands-on review. It’s a side-by-side of what Logitech itself publishes for each model, checked on 12 November 2026. That turned out to be useful, because a lot of what gets repeated about these two mice is wrong.

The short answer

Buy the MX Master 4 if you want the haptic thumb panel, the Actions Ring shortcuts and a Logi Bolt receiver in the box. Buy the MX Master 3S for Mac if you only use Bluetooth, want the lighter of the two, and would like a charging cable included.

On battery life, scrolling, sensor and the number of computers each one can pair with, Logitech lists the same figures for both. Those shouldn’t decide it.

Two claims worth correcting

Device count. You’ll see “up to 4 devices” quoted for the MX Master 4. Logitech’s own page says it connects with up to three devices, switched with the Easy-Switch buttons or from the Actions Ring. That’s the same count as the 3S for Mac.

Quiet clicks. The 3S for Mac is sold on its Quiet Clicks, with Logitech quoting 90% less click noise. The MX Master 4 has quiet clicks too: Logitech says it has 90% less sound power on the left and right click than the MX Master 3. So “get the 3S if you want a quiet mouse” doesn’t hold up.

What the MX Master 4 adds

The headline change is the Haptic Sense Panel under your thumb. It gives a small physical tap for things like snapping to a grid, switching desktops or a notification, and you set the strength in Logi Options+ (Windows and macOS).

Then there’s the Actions Ring, an on-screen menu of shortcuts that opens at the cursor. Logitech’s figure of “up to 33%” time saved comes from its own 2025 study of 37 MX Master users, so treat it as a maker claim rather than a measured result.

The other practical difference is the receiver. The MX Master 4 ships with a USB-C Logi Bolt receiver. If Bluetooth is locked down on your work laptop, or you simply prefer a dongle, that’s one less thing to buy. The 3S for Mac works with Bolt as well, but you’d buy the receiver separately.

What the 3S for Mac still does well

It’s 9 g lighter and a little smaller in every direction except depth. It comes in Mac-inspired colours and Logitech pitches it at macOS and iPadOS first. And it includes a USB-C to USB-C cable, which the MX Master 4 doesn’t. If you’re pairing it with a MacBook, an iMac and an iPad over Bluetooth, it covers that without the extra parts.

Who should pick which

  • Pick the MX Master 4 if you live in apps where shortcuts and haptics would earn their keep (design, video, spreadsheets), or you need a receiver.
  • Pick the MX Master 3S for Mac if Bluetooth is fine for you, you’d rather have the lighter body, and you don’t care about haptics.
