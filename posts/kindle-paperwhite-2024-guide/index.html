The question with an e-reader isn’t “is it good” — it’s whether you’ll read more on it than on the phone you already own. The Kindle Paperwhite’s case is mostly about what it doesn’t do. Here is what Amazon lists for the current 16 GB model, and who it suits.

Who should buy it

  • You read in bed. The white-to-amber adjustment is aimed squarely at night reading.
  • You travel. Weeks of battery means you don’t pack a charger for a short trip.
  • You read by the pool or in the bath. Waterproofing is a listed feature, not an add-on.
  • Your phone keeps winning. The listing’s strongest pitch is the lack of apps and notifications.

Who should skip it

  • You mostly read PDFs, textbooks or comics. A 7-inch greyscale screen is designed for flowing text.
  • You read a couple of books a year. Your phone is probably enough.
  • You want a UK link from this page. I could only verify this exact listing on Amazon.in.

What the listing doesn’t tell you

  • Ads on the lock screen. Some Kindle variants show offers; check the live page for the exact configuration you’re buying.
  • Cover and charger. Assume neither is included unless the box contents say so.
  • “12 weeks” is Amazon’s estimate under its own usage assumptions. Heavy nightly reading with the light up will be shorter.

Bottom line

If you read most days and your phone keeps pulling you away, the Paperwhite’s distraction-free design and long battery are the reasons to buy. If you read mostly PDFs or rarely read at all, skip it.
