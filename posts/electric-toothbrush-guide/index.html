The two features that change brushing habits are a pressure sensor (stops you scrubbing your gums) and a two-minute timer with 30-second prompts. Most mid-range electric toothbrushes have both; the real choice is rotating head (Oral-B) versus sonic (Philips), and how much you’ll pay for extra modes. This guide compares four.

Oral-B Pro 3

Oral-B lists three modes, a pressure sensor that alerts you when you push too hard, and a two-minute timer split into four 30-second quadrants. Pick it if you like the feel of a small round rotating head. Skip it if you prefer a quieter sonic brush.

Oral-B iO3

The iO platform pairs a round head with “tuft-in-tuft” bristles and micro-vibrations, plus Oral-B’s iO pressure sensor, three smart modes and IPX7 water resistance. Pick it for Oral-B’s newer tech without top-tier extras. Skip it if brush-head cost matters — iO heads are a separate system from Pro heads.

Philips Sonicare 4100

Philips lists a pressure sensor, two intensity settings for sensitive gums and SmarTimer with QuadPacer. Pick it for a sonic brush with the two key habit features.

Philips Sonicare 1100

The entry model lists the QuadPacer timer, a slim handle, a gradual power ramp-up for new users and a two-week battery. No pressure sensor is listed. Pick it as a first electric brush. Skip it if you know you brush too hard.

What the listings don’t tell you

  • Brush-head cost over time often exceeds the handle price over a few years. Check replacement packs before choosing.
  • Plaque claims are maker comparisons against manual brushing, not against each other.
  • UK plug. The UK iO3 lists a 2-pin bathroom plug.

Bottom line

Brush too hard? Get a pressure sensor: Pro 3, iO3 or Sonicare 4100. Just starting out: Sonicare 1100.
