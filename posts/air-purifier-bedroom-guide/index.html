Reading the room-size claims

Notice Philips gives two numbers for the AC0920: 65 m² under one standard and 30 m² under another. That gap is the point, room-size claims depend on the test method. Use CADR to compare, and size up if you’re between rooms.

Levoit Core 300

Levoit lists 240 m³/h CADR, H13 True HEPA, and a range of swap-in filters for VOCs, smoke and pet allergens. Pick it for a small bedroom or if you want the smoke filter option. Skip it for large rooms.

Philips AC0920

Philips lists 250 m³/h, its NanoProtect HEPA, a 20.5 dB lowest setting and an AeraSense sensor that shows real-time air quality on the unit, with on-device controls, no app needed. Pick it if you want to see what the air is doing. Note: the UK page under this ASIN shows a different model (AC0950), so I’ve linked India only.

Philips 900 Series

Same 250 m³/h CADR, a 3-layer HEPA filter, 20.5 dB sleep mode with a dimmed display, and a listed maximum draw of 23 W. Pick it if you’re in the UK or want the dimmed display at night.

What the listings don’t tell you

  • Filter replacement cost and interval: check the filter part number and its price before buying the purifier.
  • Noise at high speed. Only the lowest setting is quoted.
  • Door open vs closed. Coverage figures assume a closed room.

Bottom line

For most bedrooms, a 250 m³/h unit gives headroom: AC0920 in India, 900 Series in India or the UK. Levoit Core 300 fits smaller rooms and filter-specific needs.
