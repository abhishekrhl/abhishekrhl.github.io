If you want better coffee without a machine, the real choice is batch versus single cup: a French press makes several mugs at once but leaves fine sediment; an AeroPress makes one to three cups through a paper filter. This guide helps you pick between the classic Bodum Chambord in two sizes and the travel-sized AeroPress Go.

## Bodum Chambord — the batch brewer

Bodum's listings describe a stainless-steel frame around a heat-resistant borosilicate glass beaker. The 8-cup page warns it is **not for stovetop use** and that you turn the lid to close the spout; the 4-cup page lists it as dishwasher-safe. Glass is the trade-off: easy to see and clean, but it can break, so check that replacement beakers are sold in your region before you buy.

**Pick this if** you make coffee for more than one person, or you like a heavier-bodied cup. **Skip if** sediment at the bottom of the mug annoys you.

## AeroPress Go — the single-cup traveller

AeroPress lists the Go as making one to three cups per press, with a mug and lid that pack the brewer inside, and 350 paper micro-filters in the box. The maker's own pitch is "no grit in your cup like with a French press" — that is the paper filter doing its job. It also claims cold brew in two minutes; treat that as the maker's method, not a lab result.

**Pick this if** you brew one cup at a time, at a desk or on trips. **Skip if** you regularly serve three or more people.

## What the listings don't tell you

- **Grind matters more than the brewer.** French press wants coarse grounds; AeroPress tolerates finer. A blade grinder or pre-ground "filter" coffee will change results more than which device you buy.
- **Ongoing costs.** The AeroPress uses paper filters (350 included); the Chambord uses a reusable mesh.
- **Cleaning.** A French press needs the grounds scooped out; an AeroPress ejects a puck.

## Bottom line

Batch and body: Chambord, sized to your household. One clean cup, anywhere: AeroPress Go.
