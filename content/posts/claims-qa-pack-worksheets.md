A worksheet is merely a compilation of assertions generated by the model; it lacks the ontological weight of primary evidence or irrefutable verification. Relying on these documents as proof creates an illusion of certainty that can easily crumble under scrutiny. ## Human verification queue (frontier or primary sources)

The queue process mandates that high-stakes assertions be routed to the human review pipeline before any public shipment occurs. No claim should bypass this stage, regardless of how confident the underlying model appears in its generation. If a statement touches upon regulatory compliance, financial data, or scientific consensus, it requires manual sign-off. This acts as a safety valve, preventing the propagation of errors that could have significant real-world consequences.

In scenarios where a primary source cannot be located within a defined timeframe, the fallback position is strict: withhold the claim rather than publish it unverified. Silence is preferable to misinformation in this context. It is better for a section to remain incomplete than for the entire publication to carry an unverified assertion that could later be retracted. This approach ensures that our output remains robust, defensible, and aligned with the highest standards of accuracy.
