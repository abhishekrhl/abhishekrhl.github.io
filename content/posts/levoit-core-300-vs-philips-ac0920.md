These are two compact purifiers that people shortlist for a bedroom. On paper they look far apart, mostly because the makers rate room size in different ways. Read like for like, they're closer than the headline numbers suggest. We used Levoit's and Philips' own product pages, checked on 12 November 2026, and we haven't run either unit ourselves.

## The short answer

Pick the **Philips AC0920** if you want to see the air quality on the unit and let it set its own fan speed. It has a PM2.5 sensor, an Auto mode, a sleep mode Philips rates at 20.5 dB, and a filter Philips says lasts up to a year.

Pick the **Levoit Core 300** if smells matter as much as dust. Its standard filter includes an activated carbon stage for cooking smells, smoke and pet odours, and Levoit sells pet, smoke and toxin versions of the replacement filter.

Neither model has Wi-Fi or app control listed. More on that below, because it's where a lot of comparisons go wrong.

## Room size: read the small print

The 1,066 sq ft figure you'll see quoted for the Levoit is real, but it's at one air change per hour. That means the purifier moves the room's volume of air once an hour. For a bedroom, Levoit's other figure is more useful: 222 sq ft at 4.8 air changes an hour.

Philips states a CADR of 250 m³/h, recommends the AC0920 for rooms up to 30 m², and says it can clean a 20 m² room in under 12 minutes. It doesn't say how many air changes the 30 m² figure assumes. So you can't line the two numbers up exactly, but it does mean "the Levoit is for big rooms, the Philips for small ones" isn't a fair reading. For a typical bedroom, both are in range.

## The smart features mix-up

Levoit's Core 300-P page mentions Amazon Alexa, Google Assistant and the VeSync app. Read it closely and that section is about the **Core 300S-P**, the Wi-Fi version sold alongside it. The Core 300-P itself has no app or voice features listed. If voice control matters, look for the "S" model instead.

The Philips page has a similar wrinkle. One marketing line says air quality shows "on the product display or in the app", but Philips' own FAQ on the same page is clear: the AC0920 has no Wi-Fi and no app, and everything is done from the buttons. We've gone with the FAQ.

## What the Philips does well

Its AeraSense sensor reads PM2.5 and shows the air quality on the unit as a colour and a number, and in Auto mode it picks the fan speed itself. Sleep mode dims the display as well as dropping to 20.5 dB, which matters if the purifier sits by the bed. Philips says it draws 21 W at full power and the filter (FY0900/30) lasts up to a year, with an on-screen reminder when it's due. It's also ECARF certified as allergy-friendly.

One honest note from the Philips page itself: a buyer review points out that the display tops out at 500, so very bad air reads as 500 however bad it really is. That's worth knowing if you live somewhere with heavy winter smog.

## What the Levoit does well

The activated carbon stage is the difference. Levoit says it helps with smoke, fumes, VOCs and household smells such as cooking and pets. If odour is your main complaint, that counts for more than a few decibels. You can also swap the standard filter for Levoit's Pet Allergy, Smoke Remover or Toxin Absorber versions. Levoit lists it as CARB compliant, FCC certified and ETL listed.
